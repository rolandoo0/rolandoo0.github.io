deep dive · one investigation, start to finish · tax-filing operations at a payments company

Why did filings slip?

A tax-filing team watched its on-time filing rate (OTFR) slide and wanted to know why, how sure we could be, and how to catch it sooner next time. Scroll the chart: every dot is one real filing.

Data
336 filings · 5 merchants · 44 error events · 12 weeks
Method
SQL (18 queries), independent pandas re-check of all 69 figures
Rebuilt in
dbt · DuckDB → Snowflake · Airflow
In [1] · size it

336 filings. 30 missed.

Each column is a week, 28 filings tall. Volume never moved, so capacity isn't the story. OTFR averaged 92.9% in weeks 1–4 and 90.2% in weeks 5–12.

fct_period_summary

In [2] · find the shape

The average hides a cliff.

A 2.7-point drop sounds mild. Weekly, OTFR fell to 71.4% in week 7. One filing moves a week by 3.6 points, so I read trends on a pooled rolling 3-week rate, which bottomed at 85.7%.

fct_weekly_otfr · otfr_rolling

In [3] · segment it

Only v2 broke.

Split by reporting API, v1 never dropped below 90.5% after week 5. v2's rolling rate fell to 76.2%. Two v2 merchants that joined in different years started failing in the same week, which points to a platform change, not a merchant problem.

fct_weekly_otfr_by_api

In [4] · name the cause

One new failure mode.

validation_error didn't exist before week 5. It caused 16 of the 22 missed filings since (73%). It takes ~61 hours to fix, against ~10 for auth failures, so with weekly deadlines 76% of them landed late.

fct_error_type_summary · fct_missed_by_cause

In [5] · test the claim

Remove it and the drop disappears.

Treat those 16 misses as on time and weeks 5–12 land at 97.3%, above the baseline. I also ruled out volume, merchant mix, a state rule change and noise. Confidence: moderate, since the v2 signal rests on two merchants.

fct_period_summary · otfr_without_validation_errors

In [6] · catch it next time

Two weeks earlier.

OTFR is a lagging metric. An error-level alert calibrated only on weeks 1–4 fires in week 5. The OTFR alert confirms in week 7. Replayed out of sample, the early rule buys two weeks, and it keeps firing in week 11 after the headline recovered.

At my 87.8% calibration, a headline-only alert also trips in week 4, a normal baseline week. That is why my real confirmed-impact rule also requires an error type running at 2× its baseline, and why the early-warning rule watches errors instead of OTFR.

With the threshold at 87.8%, the OTFR alert first fires in week 7 with no false alarms; the early-warning rule fires in week 5.

What I told leadership

The decline came from one new failure mode: validation errors on filings sent through the v2 reporting API. Without them, weeks 5–12 would have beaten the baseline. The headline has recovered, but the error hasn't stopped, so the recovery is fragile.

Treat validation errors as priority tickets, add error-level alerting, then confirm the root cause with v2 release logs, the failing field in the error messages, and a full-population v1 vs. v2 comparison.

Bonus question: I designed an A/B test for an automated retry, randomized per failed filing so both affected merchants land in both arms, with filing accuracy and duplicate submissions as guardrails.

RULE 1New failure mode

An error type with a near-zero baseline appears for 2+ merchants in a week. Goes to engineering and ops. Fired weeks 5, 7, 9, 11.

RULE 2Confirmed impact

OTFR below baseline − 1 SD (87.8%) and an error type at 2× its baseline across 2+ merchants. Goes to the ops lead and leadership. Fired weeks 7 and 9.

RULE 3At-risk filing

An open error whose deadline is closer than that error type's typical fix time gets bumped up the queue before it turns into a miss.

Then I made it a pipeline

An investigation done by hand gets done once. I rebuilt this one as a dbt project so the same numbers refresh every morning and a failing data test stops a bad input before it reaches a dashboard.

otfr_analytics

Three raw tables become typed staging views, one enriched filing-level model, and seven marts. A pooled-rate macro keeps every percentage a sum over a sum, never an average of averages. Alert thresholds are project variables, calibrated on the baseline and replayed on later weeks.
